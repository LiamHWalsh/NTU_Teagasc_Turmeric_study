# Manuscript-Ready Methods and Results (Exploratory Small-N Analysis)

## Methods
We performed an exploratory, effect-size-focused paired analysis of microbiome taxonomy and pathway profiles in a low-sample setting. For each feature, participant-level change was defined as 6-month abundance minus baseline abundance, and Treatment versus Control groups were compared on these within-participant deltas.
Feature inclusion followed a prevalence-and-abundance filter (taxonomy: prevalence >= 0.20 and mean abundance >= 0.001; pathways: prevalence >= 0.20 and mean abundance >= 1.0 CPM). Primary analysis used the raw scale for interpretability, with log1p transformation used as directional sensitivity analysis; taxonomy was further assessed using CLR-transformed abundances to evaluate compositional robustness.
For each retained feature, we estimated mean and median delta differences, Cohen's d, and probability of superiority, and quantified uncertainty with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (2,000 resamples). Participant-level sensitivity was assessed via leave-one-participant-out (LOO) re-estimation, summarized by sign agreement and sign-flip behavior. Features were prioritized by a transparent consensus ranking that combined absolute effect magnitude, CI width, and LOO directional stability.
Exploratory taxonomy-function integration was then performed by pairing top-ranked genera and pathways and calculating Spearman correlations on participant-level deltas. For each pair, we reported bootstrap correlation intervals (2,000 resamples), permutation p-values (2,000 permutations), and BH-adjusted q-values as multiplicity context. The analytical framework was designed for hypothesis prioritization and does not constitute confirmatory feature-level inference.

## Results
The paired analysis set included 11 participants (Treatment 8, Control 3). Feature panels included 29 strain-resolved entries (29 unique strain names), 5 genera, and 339 pathways.
At panel level, median absolute effect sizes were 0.71 for strain, 0.83 for genera, and 0.67. Confidence intervals excluded zero for 13/29 strain features (44.8%), 2/5 genus features (40.0%), and 109/339 pathway features (32.2%).
Directional robustness across raw and log1p scales was high (strain 1.00, genus 1.00, pathway 0.94), and median LOO sign agreement was 1, supporting stability of direction under participant exclusion.
Precision differed substantially across feature levels: median CI width was 0.79 for strain, 9.26 for genus (11.8x strain), and 106.45 for pathways (135.4x strain). Among larger effects (|d| >= 0.8), directionality was mixed for strain (+4/-9) and genera (+1/-3), and pathways (+64/-80).

Feature-level interpretation indicated coherent but exploratory signals across taxonomic and functional strata.
At strain level, SGB1934 showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-1.40, mean delta difference=-0.33, 95% bootstrap CI -0.59 to -0.08, LOO sign agreement=1.00). Direction was consistent under CLR sensitivity (d_CLR=-0.86). SGB2290 showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-1.04, mean delta difference=-0.17, 95% bootstrap CI -0.40 to -0.01, LOO sign agreement=1.00). Direction was consistent under CLR sensitivity (d_CLR=-0.68). SGB2328 showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-0.85, mean delta difference=-0.14, 95% bootstrap CI -0.31 to -0.01, LOO sign agreement=1.00). Direction was consistent under CLR sensitivity (d_CLR=-0.90).
At genus level, Blautia showed a greater increase in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=1.92, mean delta difference=9.65, 95% bootstrap CI 5.34 to 14.60, LOO sign agreement=1.00). Direction was consistent under CLR sensitivity (d_CLR=1.30). Alistipes showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-1.76, mean delta difference=-5.19, 95% bootstrap CI -8.06 to -2.57, LOO sign agreement=1.00). Direction was consistent under CLR sensitivity (d_CLR=-1.35). Parabacteroides showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-0.75, mean delta difference=-0.37, 95% bootstrap CI -0.88 to 0.19, LOO sign agreement=1.00). Direction was consistent under CLR sensitivity (d_CLR=-0.61).
At pathway level, PWY66-389: phytol degradation showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-1.34, mean delta difference=-2.67, 95% bootstrap CI -5.11 to -0.43, LOO sign agreement=1.00). REDCITCYC: TCA cycle VI (Helicobacter) showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-1.08, mean delta difference=-3.41, 95% bootstrap CI -6.40 to -0.42, LOO sign agreement=1.00). PWY-7254: TCA cycle VII (acetate-producers) showed a greater decrease in treatment relative to control (Cohen's d=-1.11, mean delta difference=-4.45, 95% bootstrap CI -8.43 to -1.11, LOO sign agreement=1.00).

Cross-method triangulation for Eubacterium rectale supported prioritization but not confirmation.
Eubacterium rectale was not present in the effect-size panel after filtering.
In MaAsLin3, Eubacterium rectale showed prevalence model coef=13.55, q_joint=1.2e-12; abundance model coef=0.86, q_individual=1e+00. The prevalence model includes a small-random-effects warning in the MaAsLin output.
Taken together, these results suggest that Eubacterium rectale is a biologically plausible follow-up target, but inference remains provisional in this small paired dataset.

In exploratory taxonomy-function integration, 100 genus-pathway pairs were evaluated. 5 pairs had |rho| >= 0.80 and 25 had permutation p < 0.05. 13 pairs met BH q < 0.10 (minimum q=2.5e-02).
Correlation magnitudes were moderate overall (median |rho|=0.41; maximum |rho|=0.92). Top-ranked associations included Blautia with superpathway of glycerol degradation to 1,3-propanediol (rho=0.92, 95% bootstrap CI 0.65 to 1.00, permutation p=5e-04, BH q=2.5e-02); Alistipes with guanosine nucleotides degradation III (rho=-0.83, 95% bootstrap CI -0.97 to -0.41, permutation p=4e-03, BH q=8e-02); Alistipes with 2-oxobutanoate degradation I (rho=0.89, 95% bootstrap CI 0.56 to 1.00, permutation p=1e-03, BH q=3.33e-02); Alistipes with TCA cycle V (2-oxoglutarate synthase) (rho=0.82, 95% bootstrap CI 0.30 to 0.99, permutation p=3e-03, BH q=7.5e-02); Blautia with superpathway of pyrimidine ribonucleosides degradation (rho=0.76, 95% bootstrap CI 0.29 to 0.97, permutation p=1e-02, BH q=9.23e-02).
Overall, the analysis supports candidate ranking and mechanistic hypothesis generation, while emphasizing that independent replication is required before confirmatory conclusions.
